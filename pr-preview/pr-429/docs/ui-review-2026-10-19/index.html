UI review · desktop / TV

Tandemonium screen flow review

Build main @ 467dc8bScreen 1920×1080, desktop-app profileControllers DualSense · Steam Controller via Puck (WebHID) · Switch Pro · GameSir Super Nova · XboxDate 19 Oct 2026

I drove every lobby step, popup and ride state with 1–4 simulated controllers and captured each screen. The core flows work. On a TV they mostly fall down for three reasons: the lobby is a phone-width column that overflows 1080p, the Back hint and controller glyphs are handled differently on every screen, and several steps put focus somewhere the player can’t see.

Findings

Ranked by impact on a TV player

High means a player gets stuck, can’t read it, or loses their seat. Each finding links to the screens it’s about.

High

1Level select puts START RIDE below the screen and keeps focus on it

TV readabilityBug

At 1920×1080 the level list is 1345px tall. Difficulty sits at y=1069 and START RIDE at y=1218, both below the fold. START RIDE holds controller focus anyway, so the player sees no highlight. D-pad ↓ didn’t move focus or scroll. Same in versus.

Direction Fit the step to the screen. Put cards in a row or grid on wide screens, and keep difficulty and START RIDE in a fixed action bar. Scroll the focused item into view.

High

2Versus: the last player to join becomes Player 1

BugControllers

With several controllers connected at launch, the game leaves P1 empty on purpose. In versus the DualSense pressed ✕ first and was seated as P2, then the Steam Controller P3 and the Switch Pro P4. The Super Nova pressed last and filled the empty P1 seat.

Direction Seat players in join order: first ✕/A is Player 1. Check on real hardware; this ran with simulated pads.

High

3Captain screen breaks with 2+ controllers

TV readabilityBugControllers

With DualSense, Switch Pro and Steam Controller connected: the room code is drawn over the bike preview, and the three JOIN AS PLAYER 2 buttons run under the footer. It says “Player 1: —” and “2nd controller detected!” with three connected. The join buttons use the danger red.

Direction Use the versus controllers list here too, with the same seat states. Name the captain’s controller, and give joining the positive (accent) style.

High

4Versus join instructions are unreadable

TV readabilityControllers

The hint under the teams is 9.9px black text on the dark card. It also hard-codes “Press A … B leave your seat” whatever controller P1 holds (✕/◯ on a DualSense). Team titles are 12px.

Direction Make this the step’s main instruction, at full size, with the controller’s real glyphs.

High

5The Back hint overlaps other controls

BugInconsistency

“◯ Back” is positioned on its own and lands on top of whatever sits there: JOIN AS PLAYER 2 on the captain screen, the P4 row in versus, CHOOSE LEVEL in the browser versus, and the footer in the room.

Direction Give every step one footer row for its Back/hint, laid out with the step, not absolutely positioned.

High

6First-time SOLO drops straight into the tutorial

Flow

A player with no tutorial on record who presses SOLO RIDE lands in a 3-2-1 countdown with no screen saying what’s happening. SKIP TUTORIAL is small, top-right, and shows no button glyph. A returning player gets level select instead, so the same button does two different things.

Direction Show level select with the Tutorial card focused and labelled “Start here”. Or show one short interstitial (“First ride: a 60-second tutorial · ✕ Start · ◯ Skip”).

High

7Unplugging the controller mid-ride does nothing

ControllersFlow

The DualSense was removed during a solo ride. The ride kept going with no pause and no “Reconnect your controller” prompt. Plugging it back in also showed nothing.

Direction Pause and show a reconnect overlay naming the seat and controller. Resume on any button from that controller.

Medium

8Type is too small for a TV, and the footer is the biggest text

TV readability

Main buttons are 18px, card descriptions and hints 10–14px, end-screen buttons smaller still. The footer credits are 35px and the version label 29px, the largest text on the lobby. The Tandemonium logo and subtitle are hidden at this resolution.

Direction Set a 10-foot type scale (body ≥ 24px at 1080p, primary actions ≥ 28px). Shrink the footer, and bring the logo back in place of the bike name.

Medium

9Lobby is a narrow phone-style column on a 16:9 screen

TV readabilityInconsistency

Every lobby step shares one ~970px column under a bike preview that takes ~45% of the height. The side art is decoration only. Steps that need room (level select, captain, versus) overflow because they compete with the carousel.

Direction On wide screens, show the bike on one side and the step on the other. Or hide the carousel on steps that aren’t about the bike.

Medium

10Initial focus lands somewhere different on every step

InconsistencyControllers

Captain, room and versus all open with focus on the Help “?” toggle. Level select focuses the off-screen START RIDE. Game over focuses SAVE CLIP instead of RESTART. The focus ring on round toggles is about twice as thick as on buttons.

Direction One rule: focus the step’s primary action. One focus-ring style for every focusable.

Medium

11◯ closes every popup except the Garage

InconsistencyControllers

Help, leaderboard, profile, settings, phone and credits all closed on ◯. The Garage stayed open. It also uses its own small centered layout over the 3D scene, unlike every other screen.

Direction Treat the Garage as a step or a sheet with the shared Back behaviour.

Medium

12Start opens Settings, not a pause menu

FlowControllers

Start (Options on a DualSense) mid-ride opened the Settings list over the running ride. No Resume / Restart / End ride menu appeared, and Create/View did nothing visible.

Direction Map Start to a pause menu: Resume, Restart, Settings, End ride. Pause the ride while it’s open in solo.

Medium

13Button glyphs are right for DualSense and Xbox only

ControllersInconsistency

DualSense shows ✕/◯ and Xbox shows A/B. Switch Pro and the Steam Controller fall back to a generic 4-dot diamond, though the Steam Controller has A/B printed on it. The ride hint says “alternate LB and RB (or LT/RT)” even with a DualSense (L1/R1). The Super Nova shows ✕/◯ because in DS4 mode it reports as a DualShock 4; it can’t be told apart.

Direction One glyph component fed by the controller registry, with Steam Controller → A/B and Switch → B/A-position glyphs. Use it in every hint.

Medium

14The Puck’s Steam Controller is labelled “Direct USB-C”

ControllersBug

In the versus controllers list and the captain join buttons, a Steam Controller connected through the Puck (28de:1304) reads “STEAM CONTROLLER 2026 (DIRECT USB-C)”, the name of the 28de:1302 entry.

Direction Name it from the WebHID product id, not the shared gamepad-id pattern.

Medium

15End screens each lay out their actions differently

InconsistencyTV readability

Game over puts five differently styled buttons in one row (SAVE CLIP, RESTART, END RIDE, GARAGE →, SEND A LINK…). Victory splits a giant headline from small stacked buttons. Tutorial complete puts a Steam store widget between the results and its buttons. Demo end and Partner left use tiny centered buttons.

Direction One result-screen layout: headline, stats, then one action bar with the same order and the primary action first.

Flow

In the desktop app the first-launch veil keeps saying “Loading Tandemonium…” once loading is done. It waits for a button press but never asks for one.

Direction Switch the label to “Press any button” (with the glyph) when boot completes.

Reusable controls

Where one component should replace many

The lobby steps already use @usersfirst/ui (uf-flow, uf-step, uf-button, uf-seat). The in-ride overlays and popups mostly don’t. index.html has 57 uf-buttons and 100 raw <button>s across 25 class names, and game.js builds 7 more by hand.

Button inventory today

57 uf-button (14 accent · 12 ghost · 1 versus)35 opt-btn9 lobby-toggle9 qm-item5 side-btn5 ping-btn4 vol-btn4 profile-popup-*3 clip-btn3 lobby-credits-btn3 difficulty-btn2 bike-carousel-nav2 tourist-*7 built in game.js

uf-glyph: one controller-glyph component

  • Glyph logic lives in 8 places in lobby.js. The ride hints in hud.js and game.js hard-code “LB/RB”, and the versus hint hard-codes “A/B”.
  • Feed it from the controller registry so the Back hint, spinner hints, versus join line, ride hints and SKIP TUTORIAL all agree (fixes finding 13).

Step footer slot

  • A uf-step footer row holding Back + the controller hint, laid out with the step.
  • Removes every overlap in finding 5 and gives each step one place to look for “how do I go back”.

Controller list / seat

  • Versus has a good controllers list (CONNECTED → SEATED). The captain step builds its own red JOIN buttons for the same job.
  • One component for both, and for the lab lobby, using uf-seat states (findings 3, 14).

Result screen + action bar

  • Game over, victory, tutorial complete, demo end, partner left and versus results each lay out actions differently.
  • One layout: headline, stats, one action bar with the primary action first and focused (findings 10, 15).

Sheet / modal shell

  • Help, leaderboard, profile, settings, phone, garage and disconnect each have their own close button and focus handling.
  • One shell with a focus trap, ◯/B and Esc to close, and TV-size type (findings 11, 19).

Icon button

  • The round lobby toggles (lobby-toggle), carousel arrows and footer icons are three different kinds of icon button.
  • One uf-icon-button with the same focus ring as uf-button (finding 10).
Keep

Decisions worth building on

  • Focus is easy to see. The green ring on buttons reads clearly from across a room.
  • DualSense and Xbox glyphs are right. ✕/◯ and A/B appear in the hints and the code spinner.
  • The versus controllers list shows every controller with its state and team, the model the captain step should copy.
  • Code entry by spinner lets a stoker join a room without a keyboard.
  • The Steam Controller over the Puck navigates menus through WebHID, with no Steam Input needed.
  • Level cards show your record (“No ride yet · at 0:58”), giving each ride a goal before you start.
Method

How this was captured, and what it didn’t cover

Headless Chrome on the RTX 5070 Ti at 1920×1080, with the desktop app’s user agent so the game takes its desktop-app path. Controllers were simulated: a fake Gamepad API for DualSense, Switch Pro, Super Nova and Xbox, and a fake WebHID Puck sending Steam Controller 0x45 state reports. A few states (game over, victory, demo end, partner left, tutorial complete, versus finish) were triggered through the game’s own functions rather than ridden.

Not covered: real hardware and Steam Input (the desktop app’s window.steam bridge isn’t present), a real online partner (the room is shown empty), Tourist (needs a Maps key), Slingshot runs and the daily-mode chooser. Re-check findings 2 and 7 on real controllers before fixing.
